Research

Working papers

Multi-Market Firms: Markups, Size, and Misallocation (JMP)

Abstract: Large firms are believed to hold the most market power, and theories of oligopoly attribute market power to a firm’s share of its market. Using market research data that record the sales of consumer goods firms in every product category and country they sell in, matched to firm financials, I document three facts: 1) markups are flat in a firm’s average market share, 2) markups rise with the number and the size of the markets a firm is in, and 3) larger markets have more competitors, lower top shares and a lower Herfindahl-Hirschman index. A large market can have many competitors and low concentration and still hold high markups. I rationalize the facts in an oligopoly model with multi-market firms in which firms invest in customer attachment, which lowers the demand elasticity they face. The return to attachment rises with market size, so markups rise with market size at any share. In the calibrated model, 85% of the variance of markups across firm-market pairs is between markets. A subsidy on firm size removes only about 10% of misallocation, while a schedule on market size removes nearly 90%.

Labor Force Growth and Firm Dynamics in Europe

Abstract: Firm entry has declined in Europe in parallel with the United States. For the U.S., Hopenhayn, Neira, and Singhania (2022) attribute the decline to slower labor force growth. I show that the same explanation holds in Europe. Using a new dataset on firm dynamics for twelve European economies, I compute the entry rate that labor force growth implies from firm age profiles of size and survival, which pin down firm entry without solving a structural model. The predicted entry rate tracks the data in Europe as closely as in the United States, with a root mean squared error of 0.7 percentage points for the EU aggregate against 1.6 for the U.S., suggesting that firm creation in Europe responds to the same demographic forces. Unless its labor force grows faster, Europe’s entry rate will stay near its historical low.
